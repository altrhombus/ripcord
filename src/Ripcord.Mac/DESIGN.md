# Ripcord for Mac — design

Settled 2026-09-26, as step 5 of [`../../docs/macos-plan.md`](../../docs/macos-plan.md). The plan's "What makes
it a Mac app" listed working hypotheses, and this document settles them before the surfaces are built, the
way [`../../docs/design.md`](../../docs/design.md) settled the dotnet client's. Where this file says nothing,
that one's reasoning holds. Where the two disagree, this file wins on the Mac, and the disagreement is stated
here with its reason.

## The position

The dotnet client's principle carries over unchanged: **borrow the OS's behaviour, and spend identity only where
the surface is Ripcord's alone.**

On the Mac that line sits in a different place, because the OS supplies more:

- **Borrowed.** Windows, toolbars, the Settings window, sheets, menus, focus rings, full screen, Picture in
  Picture, the inspector, and Liquid Glass. The system draws glass, and Ripcord never imitates it with
  its own blur. A Ripcord surface that looks like glass *is* the system's glass (`glassEffect`), so it
  follows Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast without anyone having to remember.
- **Ours.** The console tile, the launch (the tile becoming a stream), and the pairing celebration. These are
  the three places the mark appears, and the only three.

## The mark, and no fourth hue

Unchanged from the dotnet client: **there is no Ripcord colour.** The three dashes belong to three console makers
(see [`../../brand/README.md`](../../brand/README.md), "Which dash is which"), and a fourth hue beside them
would compete with that meaning.

The Mac adds one consequence. **The app has no `AccentColor` asset.** Controls take the accent the person
chose in System Settings, because a brand accent would be both a fourth hue and a preference overruled.
The PlayStation blue appears only where it means PlayStation: on a PS5 or PS4 tile, and nowhere in chrome.

## The icon

It is generated by [`../../brand/generate-macos-icon.py`](../../brand/generate-macos-icon.py) into
`RipcordApp/Ripcord.icon`, an Icon Composer bundle, and must never be edited by hand. It holds the ground as
the fill and the mark as two groups, the wedge and the trail, so the system's glass treats them as the two
objects they are. There is no drawn edge and no drawn shape, because macOS 26 supplies both (brand README,
"The tile is layers").

**The grid was checked by compiling, not recalled.** The bundle was built with `actool` from Xcode 27 and
the rendering examined: the system applied its own shape and margin to full-canvas art, with the mark inside
them. Small sizes render from the same layers, and `ripcord-mark-small.svg` is not used. How the icon looks at
16 px in a Finder list is `[X]`: it was looked at once, not measured.

## The library

**One window, the shape of Screen Sharing's:** a grid of tiles with a toolbar, and nothing else. It is a
`Window`, not a `WindowGroup`, because a second library window has no meaning.

- **Nothing paired.** Show the mark at hero size, one sentence, and **Pair a Console…** as the one primary
  button. State the same-network limit before anyone meets it. This is not an error, so it is not an alert.
- **One or more.** A grid of tiles, sorted by name. A console on the network that is not yet paired appears
  last, as a quieter tile whose action is **Pair**. This is the dotnet client's ghost tile, made useful: it already
  knows which console is meant.

### The tile

The tile is the dotnet client's card translated. It keeps the same **gradient rule**: the direction and ratio are
Ripcord's, and the lightness belongs to the appearance. In Dark Mode the tile is deeper than the window. In
Light Mode it is lighter, reading as raised paper rather than a hole.

- **The tile holds** the console's name, the plain-text family label (PS5 or PS4, which is never
  optional), and its state in words: *Ready*, *Resting*, or *Not found*.
- **A resting console is not a fault.** The state is neutral text, never red, and connecting will wake it.
- ***Not found* keeps the play action.** It loses the family accent and nothing else, for the reason
  recorded on 2026-09-20 in the dotnet client's design document: a probe is one datagram, and silence is not knowledge.
- **The wedge sits at the tile's trailing edge and is the tile's action.** It scales with the tile.
- **Hover is a wash, and focus is the system focus ring.** They are different kinds of mark, never two
  strengths of one mark (the dotnet client's design, "Hover and focus are different kinds of mark"). The Mac supplies the focus
  ring, so it is correct in Increase Contrast for free.

**Opening a console** works the ways the Finder teaches: double-click, or select and press Return (⌘O as
well). **⌘N** pairs. The context menu holds Connect, Wake, Rename…, Show Details and Forget…. Forget asks
first, because pairing again means a trip to the console.

**Arrow keys move the selection**, so a controller can drive the window with no second focus model. Its
d-pad and left stick move the selection, A connects, and B does nothing. There is nothing to go back from in
a one-level window, and a B that closed the window would surprise people.

## The launch

**The tile becomes the stream.** The stream window opens from the tile's frame and grows to its own
size, so connecting is the tile opening rather than a new place appearing. The zoom is a window-frame
animation from the tile's rectangle on screen. With Reduce Motion it becomes a plain fade-in at the final
frame.

**Three dashes, three real stages.** The mark sits low and left in the stream window, where the picture will
not be. Its dashes fill in trail order as the session passes three stages:

| Dash | Fills when | Engine stage |
|---|---|---|
| Top (green) | The console answered | `controlOpen` |
| Middle (blue) | The session is agreed and keyed | `sessionReady` |
| Bottom (red) | The stream is up | `streamReady` |

A dash here means *distance covered*, which is the first thing the mark says. That reading does not
contradict the vendor mapping: the full-colour mark only appears once the picture has arrived.

- **Nothing is centred during connect, because the centre belongs to the game** (the dotnet client's design, "Connect: no
  ceremony").
- **One status line sits beside the mark.** There is no checklist and no time estimate, except on the
  account route. There, and only after ten seconds have passed, the line adds that this usually takes
  under a minute.
- **A resting console is woken first.** The line reads "Waking Living Room…", and no dash fills until the
  console answers.
- **Failure and reconnect reuse this composition.** Give the verdict first, then what to do, then the
  buttons. The mark dims and never turns red. The escape during a reconnect countdown is labelled **Stop
  Trying**.
- **The same progress goes wherever the session is visible:** the menu bar extra, and the Dock tile while
  connecting. Drawing the mark as a custom SF Symbol for those places is `[X]`: the symbol-template format
  must be read from the SF Symbols app when step 8 arrives, not recalled.

## The stream window

**It works like QuickTime Player,** because that is the Mac's model for a window that is mostly a picture.

- **The picture is aspect-fit on black.** The window keeps the stream's 16:9 aspect while resizing
  (`contentAspectRatio`), so there is no letterbox until the inspector opens.
- **A glass capsule floats low and centred** and holds the console name, Picture in Picture, Record, the
  inspector toggle, and Disconnect. It appears on pointer movement and leaves after three seconds of
  stillness, and the pointer hides with it. It is the system's glass.
- **The green button gives native full screen,** in its own Space, and so does ⌃⌘F. Escape leaves full
  screen, as it does in every Mac video app, while capture is off (see below).
- **Closing the window disconnects.** It does not ask by default, because a Mac window closing is not a
  surprise. "Ask before disconnecting" remains a setting.

### The inspector is the ladder

The dotnet client's three HUD rungs survive with Mac shapes:

| Rung | Question | Mac form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is anything wrong? | A single line in the capsule's place, shown automatically and cleared automatically, with hysteresis. No numbers. |
| 2 | Is it me or the network? | The top of the inspector (⌘I): a verdict, four numbers, and the codec and HDR pills. |
| 3 | What exactly is happening? | The inspector's disclosure groups below that: every field the engine reports. |

**⌘I shows and hides; it never reveals more**, for the same reason F3 does not in the dotnet client. Going deeper
is a disclosure group, which is visible, rather than a second keypress, which is not.

**The inspector claims dead space.** It is SwiftUI's trailing `inspector`, and the window widens to make
room, so it takes the pillarbox rather than covering the picture. That is the dotnet client's "rung 3 goes where the
video isn't", which the Mac provides as system behaviour. In full screen it overlays, because there is no
dead space left.

**The colour grammar for instruments is unchanged:** neutral inside the threshold and semantic once
crossed; bitrate never colours; the threshold is drawn; scales are fixed; numbers never animate.

### Capture

**Mac shortcuts always win.** ⌘W, ⌘Q, ⌘I, ⌃⌘F, ⌘-Tab and Mission Control are never sent to the console.

- **The keyboard plays only while capture is on.** Capture turns on when someone clicks into the picture,
  and turns off when the window loses key status, when the app is deactivated, or on a release shortcut.
  The shortcut is ⌃⌥ pressed together by default, and can be changed in Controls. It is a chord of
  modifiers because no game binds one on its own.
- **While capture is on, the pointer is hidden.** It is not confined yet: pointer input to the console has
  not been derived, so there is nothing to deliver it to.
- **A controller plays whenever the window is key, and in Picture in Picture.** That is the input the
  console expects, and it needs no capture.

### Picture in Picture and recording

- **Picture in Picture** comes from the display layer through `AVPictureInPictureController`, with the
  sample-buffer content source. The session continues while the window is hidden. Only video and
  controller input keep running in that state: the keyboard is not captured.
- **Recording writes the console's own video bitstream into a movie file, with no re-encode.** The
  samples the display layer receives are appended to an `AVAssetWriter` input in passthrough. Audio is
  re-encoded to AAC from the decoded PCM, because the stream's Opus has no passthrough path into a QuickTime
  movie that has been checked. Both claims are `[X]` until a recording has been played back.
- **Recordings go to the Movies folder**, named after the console and the time. A red dot on the capsule's
  Record button shows recording is on. It is the only red in the app, because a recording light is the one
  place red already means "live".

### HDR

Decode and presentation follow the display layer. The layer is told the content is HDR only when the
console says the stream is, and only on a display that reports extended dynamic range. Whether the
console's HDR metadata reaches the layer intact is `[X]`, a hardware check.

## Pairing

The dotnet client's rule, amended there on 2026-09-22, applies unchanged: **sign-in leads, and the code route is one
unweighted press away.** The route is a mechanism, and the player has no stake in it.

**Pairing is a sheet on the library.** It is not a window, because it belongs to the library and needs its
attention:

1. **Which console.** List the consoles found on the network, with *Enter an address…* for a network that
   filters broadcast (measured 2026-09-25: a PS5 that answers only unicast; journal, "The Mac streams").
   Starting from a Pair tile skips this step.
2. **Signed in.** If the console is on the account, it pairs, and the route is stated as a fact.
3. **Not signed in.** **Sign In to PlayStation Network…** is the default button. **Use a Pairing Code
   Instead** sits beside it as a plain button, with neither argued for.
4. **The code form** has the eight-digit code field and the account id field. Its caption says where the
   code appears on the console. The account id is remembered once entered, and never asked for twice.
   **Scan from the TV…** beside the code field reads the code through a camera, preferring an iPhone
   nearby as a Continuity Camera. It fills the field once two frames agree, and leaves the Pair press to
   the person.

The sheet makes no claims about account safety in either direction, for the reason recorded in the dotnet client's
design document.

**The celebration** is the largest of the three personality rooms. The full mark assembles at hero size,
the wedge first and then the trail, dash by dash. The text is "Paired." with a period. **Play Now** and
**Done** are peers, because the record is already in the Keychain by the time this appears. With Reduce
Motion, the mark appears whole.

## Settings

**A stock Mac Settings window**, with no wedge, no gradient and no Ripcord colour. Its panes are a toolbar
of tabs, in the order the dotnet client's design document settled, for the same reason (a LAN-only player never signs in):

| Pane | Holds |
|---|---|
| Picture | Resolution and frame rate, maximum bitrate, adapt automatically, video codec, HDR |
| Controls | Keyboard bindings, the capture release shortcut |
| Playing | Full screen on connect, ask before disconnecting, rest the console on disconnect, connection reporting |
| Account | PlayStation Network sign-in, consoles on this account, bring in pairings from ripcord-lab |
| Advanced | Recording location, diagnostics |

**Codec stays in Picture beside HDR**, because HDR is gated on HEVC. Each row's text says what the setting
does for the player, not what it sets.

The Account pane does not exist in the reduced edition built with `RIPCORD_BUNDLE_OAUTH_CLIENT = NO`. It is
removed, not disabled, because nothing in that edition could make it work.

## Present across the Mac

These are for step 8, settled now so that step 6 stores what they need:

- **The menu bar extra** shows the mark in one colour (`ripcord-mono.svg`) and lists the paired consoles
  with their state and Connect. While a console is connecting, its row counts the three stages. The label
  itself stays the plain mark until the custom SF Symbol exists. It is off by default and can be turned on
  in Settings, because an icon nobody asked for in the menu bar is the opposite of a good citizen.
- **The widget** shows the paired consoles as small tiles, and a tap connects. It reads a snapshot the app
  writes to an app group's container. That snapshot holds names and last-known states, never a key. The
  widget cannot read the Keychain items, and does not need to. Claiming an app group needs a provisioning
  profile, so only a signed build shares the snapshot (`RIPCORD_APP_GROUP`). An ad hoc build's widget
  shows the way into the app instead.
- **The Control Center control** is "Connect to *console*", configured per control.
- **App Intents:** Connect to Console, Wake Console, and Disconnect, over a console entity. They are
  surfaced through Shortcuts and Spotlight by an `AppShortcutsProvider`.

## Accessibility

- **VoiceOver.** A tile reads as one element: its name, family, state, and "double-tap to connect". The
  connect progress is announced once per stage, never as a running count. The inspector's numbers are
  labelled with their units.
- **Reduce Motion.** The launch zoom becomes a fade. The dashes still fill, because they are information,
  but they appear rather than grow. The celebration shows the whole mark at once.
- **Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast.** The system's glass handles both, which is why glass is
  never hand-built. Under Increase Contrast the tile's gradient becomes a flat system fill with a border, and
  the family label carries the information the colour did.
- **Text size.** The app follows the system text size and adds no scale of its own, for the reason the
  dotnet client's design document gives.

## Deliberately not designed

The About panel is the standard one. The sign-in sheet hosts a web view whose contents are not ours.
Keyboard bindings are a stock form.
