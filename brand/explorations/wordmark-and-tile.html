Wordmark and tile: what we picked

Why the wordmark and the tile look the way they do. Written 2026-09-24 to settle the ROADMAP item Settle the wordmark, and kept afterwards for the reasoning rather than as instructions — it was a page of open questions before the decisions were taken, and the arguments are the part worth keeping.

The standing answer is brand/README.md, not this page. It has the rules a later change has to obey; this has the alternatives they were chosen over. Where the two disagree, the README is right and this is out of date. Everything below shipped the same day: the word was outlined, the lockups drawn, the tile lifted and every icon regenerated from it.

Short version. Outfit 600, lowercase, set horizontally beside the mark. Keep the dark tile, lift it a little, and give it an edge on Windows and Linux only (section 5 explains why only there). Where Windows asks for an unplated icon, drop the tile there only. Every choice below names the alternative it was picked over, and the specimens are live rather than screenshots — the type is fetched from Google Fonts and the geometry is copied from brand/*.svg, so a change to the artwork shows up here.

1. Where the wordmark appears

This is what decided how much the choice mattered. Two earlier decisions already kept it off the app's own surfaces. First run shows the full mark and no wordmark, because the title bar already carries the name (ConsolesPage.xaml, design review 2026-09-21). The PS3 shell also dropped its title moment on 2026-09-17. So the wordmark lives almost entirely outside the app:

What this means for the choice: the wordmark will mostly be seen at display sizes, on a GitHub page, next to other projects' logos. How it holds up at 13 px matters much less than whether it has its own character at 40–60 px.

2. The typeface

All three shortlisted faces are SIL OFL 1.1. OFL allows outlining a face into artwork with no attribution in the artwork itself. We would commit the outlined paths, not the font, so the build takes on no font dependency. Each face below is shown at display size on both grounds, in the two cases worth comparing, with a weight ladder.

For: it has the most geometric bowls of the three. The o c d p are nearly circular, which echoes the wedge's rounded joins and the dashes' pill ends. It is the face already used in the design drawings, so choosing it re-cuts nothing. It is also less common than Poppins.

Against: it has a low stroke contrast, and the r terminal is short, so at 400 it can look thin next to a mark this solid. That is why 600 is recommended.

For: it has the most personality. The tall x-height and slightly condensed set make it compact, and the angled terminals pick up the wedge's diagonal rather than its roundness. It is the best of the three for an app that wants to feel sharp rather than friendly.

Against: it is the least "rounded geometric" of the shortlist, so it matches the dashes less than the wedge. Its humanist details sit close to Segoe UI Variable, which could make the wordmark look like part of the app's UI text rather than a mark.

This is here to show what the wordmark looks like if we skip the decision. It is the app's own UI face (it only renders if your system has it). Two problems rule it out. First, a wordmark in the same face as the title bar is not a mark, just the title again. Second, Segoe is not OFL: outlining it into redistributable artwork is at best a grey area under Microsoft's font licence, and the project has enough licence arguments already.

Case: lowercase, in artwork only

Picked: lowercase. "ripcord" has a descender (p), two ascenders (i's dot, d) and no capital to fight the wedge for the left edge. Title case puts a tall R right next to the mark, and the two tallest shapes then compete. In running text the name stays "Ripcord". This is only about the artwork. Alternative: title case, if you want the artwork to match the title bar exactly. It is an easy call either way, and it does not change the generator work.

3. The lockup

A lockup is how the mark and the word are arranged together. One form is primary; a second is worth making only if a real surface needs it.

ripcord
Horizontal Primary

Mark height ≈ cap height + descender. The wedge points into the word, which suits a mark whose whole idea is motion to the right. It fits the README header, the wide tile and the splash.

ripcord
Stacked Only if needed

Useful for square spaces: social preview, a square Store hero, the PS3 title area. Skip it until one of those is actually being built. It is cheap to add later because it uses the same outlined word.

ripcord
Word alone Not recommended

Without the mark, the colour disappears and "ripcord" becomes just a word in a nice font. The mark carries the identity; the word should never appear without it in branded artwork.

Spacing rule to write into brand/README.md once chosen: the gap between mark and word equals the width of the shortest (green) dash, and the clear space around the lockup is one blue dash on every side. Both are defined by the mark's own parts, so they scale without a separate spec.

4. The tile's dark ground

Pushback: keep the ground dark. The plate is doing work that is easy to miss, and the numbers show it. But I think your instinct points at a real problem. It is just not the colour.

What the dark plate protects. Non-text graphics need 3:1 contrast against what surrounds them (WCAG 1.4.11). The dashes are the part of the mark that carries meaning.

On a light ground the green dash fails, and it is the dash brand/README.md already calls the palette's weak point. The plate is what lets the three colours stay the three colours. The README's own rule is that the ground stays out of the mark's way. The table also shows how far the plate can be lifted: about #303743, where blue and red reach 3:1.

What I think actually bothers you. The same numbers show it. The current tile against a dark Windows taskbar (#1C1C1C) has 1.07:1 contrast, so in dark mode the plate is invisible and the icon already reads as plateless. It only "shows" in light mode, where it is a solid near-black square among Windows 11 icons, most of which have no plate at all (Explorer, Settings, Edge, Terminal). The heaviness is real, but it comes from the plate being present where Windows expects none and flat where it should have an edge, not from the plate being dark.

MSIX has exactly this hook. targetsize-*_altform-unplated and _altform-lightunplated are the theme-aware, plateless icons Windows uses on the taskbar and in Start. We generate both names today, but both are rendered from the plated tile. The "unplated" assets have a plate.

  • The light variant brings back the green-dash problem (2.2:1). At taskbar size the dash lengths do the differentiating and the graphite wedge carries recognition, so this page argued for accepting it. The alternative is a light-ground green (#1F9444 reaches 3.5:1), which the README would have to approve as a second palette entry.
  • The zip build can't do this. An unpackaged exe's taskbar icon comes from AppIcon.ico, which has one image for both themes. A plateless icon there means a white wedge that disappears on a light taskbar or a graphite one that disappears on a dark taskbar. So the .ico keeps its plate, and this is the strongest single reason the plate has to exist.

The green dash fails on every size (2.4:1). It becomes a bright tile on a dark taskbar, which is louder than what we have now. It also breaks the ground-stays-out-of-the-way rule. It is the "just make it lighter" answer, and it is worse on every count.

5. The tile on macOS, Linux and Android

Added 2026-09-24, before the macOS, Linux and Android builds start. Option B's border and highlight are material, and on two of the four platforms the operating system owns the material. Android applies its own mask shape to the icon; macOS 26 puts non-standard icons inside its own rounded square and adds its own glass edge. A border drawn into the master art gets cut up on Android and doubled on macOS. So the master is split into layers, and the edge is a finish that only some generator targets add.

Neither platform crops or restyles the icon. Linux icons go into the freedesktop hicolor theme as an SVG plus PNGs, and are shown exactly as drawn. GNOME's style guide prefers plateless icons in its own style, but nothing enforces that, and a Flathub listing accepts this. The hairline earns its place here: both desktops have dark themes, and without an edge the tile disappears into them, the same way it does on a dark Windows taskbar today.

Android adaptive iconbackground = ground · foreground = mark · monochrome = mono

The last two figures are why the border can't be in the master. When the launcher's mask is applied, B's hairline is left as four slivers on a circle and as one corner on a teardrop. The same drawing in a different launcher looks broken.

  • The existing geometry already fits. brand/README.md's rule that the mark stays inside a centred ø58 circle of the 64 box is Android's safe zone: a 66 dp safe circle within a 72 dp visible area, scaled to a 64 box, is ø58.7. The mark keeps its tile placement exactly, as it does above. Only the ground grows from 72 to 108 units, to fill Android's larger background layer.
  • The themed icon is the mono cut, with no changes. The system tints it from the wallpaper, which takes away the three colours. That is the one place the dash lengths alone have to identify the mark, and ripcord-mono.svg already opened its gaps for exactly this.
  • Launchers also add parallax and a shadow, which is one more reason not to draw a highlight.
macOSground + mark on Apple's rounded square · no hairline
  • Before 26: the .icns artwork has to include Apple's rounded square, drawn at roughly 80% of the canvas with a margin, plus the conventional soft drop shadow. Our rx=15 tile only approximates that shape, and a Windows tile reused on a Mac looks slightly off, so this is its own target that reuses the ground and mark layers. There is no hairline, because a macOS icon's edge comes from its shadow.
  • macOS 26: the ground and mark go into Icon Composer as separate layers, and the system renders the glass. The right-hand figure is only my impression of that effect, to show why a drawn hairline would double up with the system's edge. It is not something we would ship. Don't judge the look from it.
  • Not verified in this session: Apple's exact grid, the 26 layer format, and how 26 treats an old-style .icns. All three come from my knowledge rather than a check. Read Apple's current icon guidance before the macOS build starts, and fix this section if it disagrees.

What this changes about option B: not the look on Windows, only where the border lives. The slate colour goes into the master, because it is what the contrast table depends on and every platform keeps it. The border and highlight become a generator step that the Windows and Linux targets add and the macOS and Android targets leave out. That also means the border is no longer a one-way door: it can be removed from one platform, or all of them, without redrawing anything.

6. Found along the way

  • The wide tile's comment contradicts its code. generate-assets.ps1 says the wide tile is "the mark on a transparent field rather than a stretched tile… a second rounded rectangle inside that reads as a sticker". It then renders ripcord-tile.svg, which is the tile, rounded rectangle included. The splash does the same. Both get replaced by the lockup anyway, so fixing the wordmark fixes this.
  • The "unplated" assets are plated, as covered under C — and they still are, since C was not taken. Worth fixing independently of everything else on this page, because the asset names promise Windows something the images don't deliver.
  • The About page hero has its own copy of the tile geometry (brand/README.md, geometry rules). Option B has to update it by hand in the same change.

7. What landed

Written as a plan and kept as a record: all of it was done on 2026-09-24, in this order. The steps were deliberately independent, so a "no" on the tile would not have blocked the wordmark.

  1. Outline the word. Render "ripcord" in the chosen face and weight to paths with fontTools (a one-off script, committed next to the output so it can be regenerated), and hand-check the kerning of rc, co and rd. Add brand/ripcord-wordmark.svg (word only, currentColor). Also record the face, weight, version and OFL notice in brand/README.md, and commit the font's OFL.txt beside it.
  2. Draw the lockups. Add ripcord-lockup.svg and ripcord-lockup-ondark.svg (horizontal) using the spacing rule above. Leave stacked out until a surface asks for it.
  3. Generator. Point the wide tile and splash at the dark lockup on a transparent field, which is what the existing comment intended. Add a README header pair (docs/ or brand/, dark and light, served with GitHub's <picture>/prefers-color-scheme).
  4. Tile (B), as layers. Add ripcord-ground.svg (the lifted gradient, with no shape) and make the generator build each platform's tile from ground + mark, adding the shape and finish itself. Windows gets the hairline and highlight. The small cut gets the lift but no hairline, because a half-pixel stroke at 16 px is mud. ripcord-tile.svg becomes a generated preview or is removed, so there aren't two sources for one drawing. Update the About hero and the card's dark gradient in Ripcord.xaml in the same commit, and re-point the design.md sentence.
  5. Unplated (C). Render the two altform assets from the bare marks (white wedge for unplated, graphite for lightunplated), and add the missing targetsizes Windows looks for (16, 24, 32, 48, 256). The .ico is unchanged.
  6. Record. Close the ROADMAP item into the journal and amend brand/README.md's "Not done yet" and palette table.

Later, per platform, when each build lands: a Linux hicolor target (the same composite as Windows); an Android target (ground as a 108-unit background, mark as foreground, mono as monochrome); a macOS target (Apple's rounded square without a hairline, and Icon Composer layers for 26). None of these needs a new drawing. This is the payoff of splitting the layers now.

The ROADMAP price (about 1 h to choose, about 2 h to draw and wire) holds for steps 1–3. B and C add roughly another hour, mostly checking assets on a real taskbar in both themes, because that is the only place C can be verified.

What was decided

  1. Face: Outfit SemiBold (600). Poppins was ruled out as too common to read as a choice; Plus Jakarta Sans sat too close to Segoe UI Variable, where the wordmark would have looked like UI text.
  2. Case: lowercase, in artwork only. In running text the name is "Ripcord".
  3. Tile: the ground lifted to #303743 → #1B1F26, with the edge added per target by the generator rather than drawn into the art — Android masks adaptive icons to a shape the launcher chooses and macOS 26 draws its own, so a drawn border survives neither. Option C, plateless unplated taskbar icons, was offered and not taken; brand/README.md lists it under Not done yet.

One thing the page got wrong about itself: it said the wordmark "lives almost entirely outside the app". The About page hero now carries it, which section 1 had listed as optional. The first-run surface still does not, for the reason given there.
